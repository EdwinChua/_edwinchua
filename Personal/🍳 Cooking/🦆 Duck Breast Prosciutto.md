A salt-cured, air-dried duck breast. Easy to make — it just requires patience.

---

## 🥩 Ingredients

- 1 high-quality duck breast (fresh or frozen)
- Sea salt — enough to fully bury the breast (e.g. Maldon)

## 🧰 Equipment

- Cheesecloth
- Butcher's twine
- A container for curing
- Space to hang in the fridge (or a sheet tray fitted with a rack)

## 👨‍🍳 Method

1. **Score the fat** in a crosshatch pattern — cut one way, then the other. Don't cut down into the meat.
2. **Bury in salt.** Cover the bottom of a container with sea salt, lay the duck breast on top, then cover completely with more salt so it's surrounded on all sides.
3. **Cure.** Cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate for **1 day**.
4. **Rinse & dry.** After a day, rinse the breast under cold water to remove all the salt, then pat dry with paper towels.
5. **Wrap.** Wrap in cheesecloth and tie with butcher's twine.
6. **Hang.** Hang in the fridge for about **7 days**, until it loses roughly **30% of its weight**. No space to hang? Lay it on a sheet tray fitted with a rack.
7. **Serve.** It's ready when firm on the outside but slightly soft. Slice thin with a sharp knife and serve.

---

> 🧪 **Doneness = weight loss.** Weigh the breast before hanging and aim for ~30% loss — that's the reliable signal, more than the day count.
